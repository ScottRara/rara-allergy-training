Module 2

Australian Allergen Laws & PEAL Labelling

Learning Objective: Understand your legal responsibilities for allergen management, including the FSANZ Food Standards Code, Plain English Allergen Labelling (PEAL), and what the law requires of food businesses.

Your Legal Obligations

Australian food law places clear responsibilities on food businesses. As a RaRa Ramen team member, you must understand and follow these requirements.

This is the Law When a customer asks about, or tells you about their food allergy, you must give them accurate information about the ingredient and allergen content of the food and drinks you prepare or sell.

What the Law Requires of RaRa Ramen

1
Provide Accurate Allergen Information When a customer asks or declares a food allergy, you must provide correct information about ingredients and allergens in the food.
2
Display or Make Available Allergen Information For unpackaged food (like ramen bowls and sides), allergen information can be provided verbally, shown in writing (menu matrix), or displayed with food.
3
Have a Trained Food Safety Supervisor All food retail businesses preparing ready-to-eat potentially hazardous food must have a trained Food Safety Supervisor who manages allergen risks.
4
Comply with Food Acts It is an offence to fail to supply food in the manner demanded by the purchaser (if the business accepts the request). Ignorance of allergen content is not a defence.

Plain English Allergen Labelling (PEAL)

The FSANZ Food Standards Code was updated on 25 February 2021 to introduce PEAL — making allergen information on food labels easier to find and understand.

What Changed with PEAL?

Before PEALAfter PEAL
Allergens could be declared anywhere on the label Allergens must appear in the ingredients list and in an allergen summary statement
Ingredient names could be technical/hard to understand Simple, plain English names must be used (e.g., "milk" not "casein")
Allergens might not stand out Allergens must be in bold font
Summary statement was optional Allergen summary statement is now mandatory
Gluten Labelling Note If wheat is an ingredient, both wheat and gluten must be listed in the summary statement. For other gluten-containing grains (rye, oats, barley), the term gluten must be used in the summary statement to help people with coeliac disease.
What does PEAL stand for?
Plain English Allergen Labelling — a requirement for simple, bold allergen declarations on food labels.
What is the FSANZ Food Standards Code?
The regulations that set the rules for food allergen labelling across Australia.
When did PEAL come into effect?
25 February 2021. Full compliance required by February 2026.

What This Means for RaRa Ramen

Since most of our food is prepared on-site and served unpackaged, here's what you need to do:

Your Daily Responsibilities

  • Know the allergen matrix: Our allergen menu matrix lists every allergen in every menu item. It must be accessible and up to date.
  • Check packaged ingredients: When receiving stock, read labels for allergen information. Look for bold allergen declarations and summary statements.
  • Check PIFs: For unpackaged ingredients (e.g., fresh noodles from suppliers), check the Product Information Form (PIF) for allergen content.
  • Watch for changes: Suppliers can change ingredients without notice. Check allergen information each time you receive new stock.
  • Answer customer questions: You must provide accurate allergen information when asked. If you don't know, say so and find out — never guess.

Product Information Forms (PIFs)

A PIF is a document from suppliers that lists ingredients and mandatory allergens in foods that don't have labels (like bulk noodles, fresh vegetables, or bakery items). At RaRa Ramen:

  • PIFs must be kept where all staff can easily access them
  • PIFs must be kept current — request updated versions from suppliers regularly
  • Check PIFs when answering customer allergen questions
Old Labels Warning Some products may still have old-style labels (without PEAL formatting) until February 2026. On these labels, allergens may not be obvious. If you are unsure about a label, check the PIF or contact the supplier.

Precautionary Allergen Labelling

You may see statements like "May contain traces of peanuts" on packaged food. These are precautionary allergen labels (PAL) and indicate that unintentional cross-contamination may have occurred during manufacturing.

For Customers with Allergies Precautionary statements are important. If a customer has a peanut allergy and a product says "may contain traces of peanuts," it is NOT safe for them. Always inform the customer and let them decide.

Case Study: Why This Matters

Real-World Scenario (Based on Actual Events)

A 13-year-old girl with allergies to peanut, crustaceans, cashew, and pistachio attended a football camp. Her allergies were declared on the registration form, and her mother spoke directly with the cook. The cook prepared safe meals but had to leave suddenly. The club president took over without being informed of the girl's allergies. Unlabelled platters were put out. The girl selected a biscuit that appeared to be chocolate chip — but it contained cashew. She experienced anaphylaxis and could not be revived.

What Went Wrong?

  • No clear written procedures for managing food allergies
  • Allergy information was not communicated when staff changed
  • Food was not labelled with allergen content
  • No separate preparation or plating for allergen-sensitive meals
  • No one checked that the girl received the correct food
Lesson for RaRa Ramen Communication must NEVER break down. If you take over from another team member, the FIRST thing you must check is: are there any allergy orders in progress? Every allergy docket must be visible, highlighted, and confirmed.

NSW Food Authority's Role

The NSW Food Authority is the regulatory body that:

  • Investigates allergen-related incidents
  • Enforces food allergen rules
  • Conducts allergen surveys and projects
  • Educates industry and consumers
  • Collaborates with local and state governments through the Food Regulation Partnership

Knowledge Check

1. Under Australian law, when must you provide allergen information to a customer?

Correct! You must provide accurate allergen information whenever a customer asks or declares a food allergy.
Incorrect. The law requires you to provide accurate allergen information whenever a customer asks or declares their allergy — regardless of cards, packaging, or who else is present.

2. What does PEAL require for allergen declarations on food labels?

Correct! PEAL requires plain English names in bold font in the ingredients list AND in a mandatory allergen summary statement.
Incorrect. PEAL (Plain English Allergen Labelling) requires simple names in bold font, listed in the ingredients and in a mandatory summary statement.

3. What is a PIF?

Correct! A PIF (Product Information Form) lists ingredients and mandatory allergens for foods that don't have labels.
Incorrect. A PIF is a Product Information Form — a document from suppliers listing ingredients and allergens for unlabelled foods.
