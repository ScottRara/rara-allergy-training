Module 4

Cross-Contamination Prevention

Learning Objective: Understand how cross-contamination (cross-contact) occurs and master practical techniques for safe storage, cleaning, handwashing, and food preparation at RaRa Ramen.

Understanding Cross-Contamination (Cross-Contact)

Cross-contamination (also called cross-contact) is the transfer of allergens from one food to another. This can happen through:

  • Direct contact: An allergen-containing food touches allergen-free food
  • Shared equipment: Using the same utensils, cutting boards, or fryer oil
  • Hands: Handling an allergen then touching other food without washing
  • Surfaces: Working on a bench that hasn't been properly cleaned
  • Splashing/spilling: Allergens splashing into nearby food during cooking
  • Airborne: Flour dust or steam carrying allergen particles
Remember Even microscopic amounts of an allergen can trigger a life-threatening reaction. Cross-contamination is the number one cause of allergic reactions in food service.

Storing Foods Safely

Proper storage prevents cross-contamination before food preparation even begins.

1
Keep storage areas clean and tidy Clean up spills immediately. If a spill contaminates other ingredients, throw away the contaminated food.
2
Store allergen-containing foods in sealed, labelled containers Label storage areas and all containers clearly so foods are always in the correct position.
3
Store high-risk items on lower shelves Items that spread or spill easily (flour, milk, sesame seeds) go in separate sealed containers on lower shelves to prevent spillage onto other items.
4
Take care when transferring foods When moving food from original packaging to storage containers, make sure other foods or containers are not contaminated. Label the new container with ingredient and allergen information.
5
Label allergen-safe meals Pre-plated or prepared meals for people with food allergies should be covered/wrapped, labelled with the customer's name and their allergies, and stored separately.

Cleaning & Sanitising for Allergen Safety

Critical Distinction Cleaning (warm water + detergent + clean cloth/paper towel) removes food allergens. Sanitising alone does NOT remove allergens. You must clean FIRST, then sanitise.

Cleaning Rules at RaRa Ramen

  • Clean equipment and surfaces with warm water, detergent, and clean cloths or paper towel before and between each food preparation task involving allergens
  • Sanitising is mandatory for food hygiene but will NOT remove allergens without prior cleaning
  • Used or dirty tea towels can cause cross-contamination — use clean cloths each time
  • Hand sanitiser does NOT remove food allergens from hands
  • Wiping hands on a tea towel does NOT remove allergens
Does sanitising remove allergens?
No. Sanitising without cleaning first does NOT remove food allergens. You must physically clean with warm water and detergent first.
Does hand sanitiser remove allergens?
No. Hand sanitiser does NOT remove food allergens. You must wash hands with soap and warm running water.

Handwashing

Hands must be washed and dried correctly before preparing food for customers with food allergies.

1
Wet hands under warm running water
2
Apply soap and lather for at least 20 seconds, including between fingers, backs of hands, and under nails
3
Rinse thoroughly under running water
4
Dry with a clean single-use paper towel or air dryer
About Gloves Gloves are only helpful for managing allergens if they are changed regularly. Washing hands with soap and warm water between tasks is more effective than wearing gloves. If you wear gloves, change them between tasks and wash hands before putting on new ones. Use non-latex gloves (some people have latex allergies).

When to Wash Hands

  • Before starting any food preparation
  • Before preparing food for a customer with food allergy
  • Between handling different ingredients/allergens
  • After touching allergen-containing foods
  • After cleaning duties
  • After using the bathroom, sneezing, coughing, or touching face/hair

Equipment & Utensils

RaRa Ramen Equipment Rules

  • Use clean equipment and utensils for each task
  • Use separate utensils for different foods (e.g., separate tongs for allergen-free dishes)
  • Have separate coloured or labelled equipment for mandatory allergens (e.g., separate toaster for gluten-free items)
  • Disassemble blenders, mixers, blades, and whisks for thorough cleaning — check carefully for food residue
  • Consider separate preparation areas for allergen-free meals
  • Check plates and cutlery are thoroughly cleaned with no food residue before serving to customers with allergies

Preparing Food Safely for Allergic Customers

Rule #1 Only staff who have been trained in food allergen management should prepare food and drinks for customers with food allergies.
1
Clean the workspace and wash hands thoroughly before starting
2
Use clean, separate utensils for allergen-free preparation
3
Check all ingredients for allergens — check every time, even for familiar products (ingredients can change)
4
Prepare the allergy meal first, before other meals, to reduce risk
5
Tell all kitchen staff that an allergy meal is being prepared so they take care not to contaminate the area or meal
6
Follow the standardised recipe exactly — no substitutions without checking allergen content
7
If in doubt, start the meal again — you cannot pick out an allergen; cross-contamination has already occurred

Watch Out For These Situations

SituationRiskAction
Oils Peanut oil, sesame oil are allergens. Shared fryer oil is contaminated. Use a different oil. Use fresh oil if the fryer has been used for allergen-containing food.
Garnishes & sauces Often contain hidden allergens (sesame seeds, fish sauce, soy) Check every garnish and sauce. Safest option: leave garnishes off allergen-free meals.
Coffee machines Steam wand retains milk residue even after cleaning. Cross-contamination between cow's milk, soy, almond, oat milk. Acknowledge to customer that complete allergen removal from steam wand is difficult.
Ingredient substitution Running out of a product and using a different brand that may contain different allergens. NEVER substitute without checking the allergen content. Update the matrix.

Standardised Recipes

Standardised recipes are recipes that everyone follows exactly, every time. No changes. No additions. This is critical for allergen management because:

  • When everyone follows the same recipe, you can be certain about the allergens in each dish
  • The allergen menu matrix stays accurate
  • Staff can give correct information to customers
  • If an ingredient changes, only the matrix needs updating (not retraining all staff on a different recipe)
Never Substitute Without Checking If you run out of an ingredient and need to use a different brand or product, you MUST check the allergen content of the replacement AND communicate the change to the Food Safety Supervisor so the allergen matrix can be updated.

Knowledge Check

1. What is the correct order for removing allergens from a surface?

Correct! Physical cleaning with warm water, detergent, and clean cloths removes allergens. Sanitising is for hygiene but does NOT remove allergens on its own.
Incorrect. You must CLEAN first (warm water + detergent + clean cloth) to remove allergens. Sanitising alone, hand sanitiser, and tea towels do NOT remove food allergens.

2. Why should allergy meals be prepared BEFORE other meals?

Correct! Preparing allergy meals first, before the workspace gets contaminated by other ingredients, significantly reduces cross-contamination risk.
Incorrect. The reason is to reduce cross-contamination risk. Working in a clean environment before other foods are prepared makes it safer.

3. You run out of your regular soy sauce and want to use a different brand. What should you do?

Correct! Different brands may have different allergen profiles. Always check, communicate, and update records.
Incorrect. Different brands of the same product can contain different allergens. You must check the replacement product, inform your supervisor, and update the allergen matrix.
