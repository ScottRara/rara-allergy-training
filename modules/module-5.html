Module 5

Communication & Emergency Response

Learning Objective: Master the communication chain between front-of-house and kitchen staff for allergy orders, understand how to handle chef cards, and know exactly what to do if a customer has an allergic reaction.

Front-of-House Staff Communication

Front-of-house (serving) staff are the first point of contact when a customer declares a food allergy. Here is the communication process you must follow:

1
Listen carefully when customers mention allergies while ordering. Help them choose appropriate foods.
2
Repeat back the customer's food allergies to make sure you have the right information.
3
Give accurate information about allergen content using the menu matrix, labels, or PIFs. This includes how food is prepared.
4
Write and highlight the food allergy on the customer's order and docket. If they have a chef card, take it with the docket to the kitchen.
5
Tell other front-of-house staff about the customer with food allergy, as well as the cook or chef.
6
Deliver the allergy meal first, before any other meals for the table, to prevent mix-ups. Check with the customer that the right meal goes to the right person.
Never Guess If you don't know the allergen content of a food, tell the customer you don't know and find out. Never guess. If an appropriate meal cannot be provided, tell the customer honestly and explain why.

Chef Cards

Some customers may present a chef card (also called an allergy card) that states their food allergies. These are available from Allergy & Anaphylaxis Australia.

  • Accept chef cards — they help communicate the customer's allergies clearly
  • Take the card with the order docket to the kitchen
  • Return the chef card with the meal when serving

Kitchen / Back-of-House Communication

1
Listen to front-of-house staff when they communicate a customer's food allergy. Confirm what the allergy is and who it is for.
2
Confirm with front-of-house that an appropriate allergen-free meal can be prepared. If not, communicate this immediately.
3
Tell all kitchen staff that a meal for a person with food allergy is being made, so everyone takes care not to contaminate the area or meal.
4
When the meal is ready: Confirm with serving staff who it is for and what allergies have been catered for. Send it to the table first to avoid mix-ups.
5
Use the red allergy identification cards provided by RaRa to identify allergen-free meals. Return any chef card with the meal.
6
If not serving immediately: Cover and label the meal with the customer's name and their allergies, and store separately.

RaRa Ramen Communication Checklist

  • Customer declares allergy to front-of-house
  • Front-of-house repeats back and confirms allergy
  • Allergy is written and highlighted on the order docket
  • Front-of-house communicates allergy to kitchen
  • Kitchen confirms the allergy and whether a safe meal can be prepared
  • Kitchen announces to all staff that an allergy meal is being prepared
  • Meal is prepared following safe procedures
  • Meal is sent out FIRST with allergy identification card
  • Serving staff confirms correct meal goes to correct person

Recognising Allergic Reactions

An allergic reaction can happen very soon after eating and can quickly become life-threatening. All staff must know how to recognise the signs.

Mild to Moderate Reaction Signs

  • Swelling of lips, face, or eyes
  • Hives or welts on the skin
  • Tingling sensation in the mouth
  • Abdominal pain or vomiting

Severe Allergic Reaction (Anaphylaxis) Signs

EMERGENCY — Any of these signs require immediate action:
  • Difficult or noisy breathing
  • Swelling of the tongue
  • Swelling or tightness in the throat
  • Wheeze or persistent cough
  • Difficulty talking or hoarse voice
  • Persistent dizziness or collapse
  • Pale and floppy (especially young children)

How to Help a Customer Having Anaphylaxis

Anaphylaxis is a MEDICAL EMERGENCY It requires immediate treatment with adrenaline (epinephrine). If treatment is delayed, anaphylaxis can be fatal.
1
Lay the person flat — do NOT allow them to stand or walk. If breathing is difficult, allow them to sit on the ground with legs outstretched.
2
Follow the customer's ASCIA Action Plan for Anaphylaxis (if they have one). If available, give an adrenaline (epinephrine) injector (EpiPen). Instructions are on the Action Plan and on the device itself.
3
Call 000 (Triple Zero) immediately. Tell them someone is having anaphylaxis.
4
Stay with the person until the ambulance arrives. Monitor their breathing. Do not leave them alone.
5
Preserve the food the customer was eating. Freeze it and label it — it may be needed for investigation.

Post-Incident Procedure

  • Report the incident internally using the RaRa Ramen incident report form
  • Report near misses as well — these help prevent future incidents
  • Conduct an internal investigation to understand what happened and prevent recurrence
  • Review and update procedures as needed
  • Promote a good food safety culture where staff feel safe reporting concerns

Encouraging Customers to Disclose Allergies

Not all customers will volunteer their allergy information. RaRa Ramen can help by:

  • Including a note on menus asking customers to inform staff of any food allergies
  • Having signage at the counter or ordering area
  • Training staff to ask if customers have any food allergies when taking orders
  • Displaying the allergen menu matrix where customers can see it

Implementing Best Practice at RaRa Ramen

Your Workplace Must Have:

  1. A food allergen management policy covering staff training, allergen knowledge, safe preparation practices, and incident procedures
  2. An allergen menu matrix for all menu items, kept up to date and accessible to all staff
  3. Ingredient lists for all products and recipes, accessible to staff
  4. Food safety monitoring tools that include allergen management in critical control point monitoring
  5. Customer communication procedures including menu notices, verbal communication protocols, and chef card handling
  6. Standardised recipes followed consistently to ensure accurate allergen information
  7. An ordering system to maintain par levels and avoid last-minute ingredient substitutions
  8. An incident reporting procedure for allergic reactions and near misses

Knowledge Check

1. A customer tells you they have a peanut allergy. What is the FIRST thing you should do?

Correct! Repeating back the allergy confirms you've understood correctly. Then write it on the docket, tell the kitchen, and help them choose safe options.
Incorrect. The first step is to repeat back the allergy to confirm you have the right information. Then proceed to write it on the docket, inform the kitchen, and help the customer choose safe options.

2. A customer is showing signs of difficult breathing, swelling of the tongue, and persistent coughing after eating. What should you do?

Correct! These are signs of anaphylaxis. Lay them flat (or seated with legs out if breathing is difficult), use their EpiPen if available, and call 000 immediately. Never allow them to stand or walk.
Incorrect. These are signs of anaphylaxis — a medical emergency. The person must be laid flat (NOT standing), their EpiPen administered if available, and 000 called immediately. Never delay treatment.

3. When should the allergy meal be sent to the table?

Correct! The allergy meal should go out first to prevent it being mixed up with other meals at the table.
Incorrect. The allergy meal must be sent to the table FIRST, before other meals, to prevent mix-ups.
