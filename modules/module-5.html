Module 5

Communication & Emergency Response

Learning Objective: Master the communication chain between front-of-house and kitchen staff for allergy orders, understand how to handle chef cards, and know exactly what to do if a customer has an allergic reaction.

RaRa Ramen Allergy Procedure

This is the mandatory allergy procedure that all RaRa Ramen staff must follow every time a guest has a food allergy. Every step matters — follow them in order, every single time.

This procedure is non-negotiable. Every team member — front-of-house, kitchen, and management — must know and follow these 10 steps.
1
Taking the Order: ALWAYS ask — “Do you have any allergies?” This question must be asked of every guest, every time. Do not wait for the guest to volunteer this information.
2
Allergy Matrix Refer to the allergy matrix to determine which dishes are safe or can be modified. If you are unsure, speak to your supervisor or chef immediately.
3
Enter Allergen Notes Enter the allergen onto the order as a note, INCLUDING severity (e.g. “anaphylactic to nuts”). Do this for ALL dishes the guest will have — not just the ones you think are affected.
4
Unclear / Complex Allergens If the allergen is unclear or complex (e.g. multiple allergens, unusual ingredients), speak to the chef directly. Never guess.
5
Red Card at the Table Place the red card stand at the guest's table. Say to the guest: “This is an allergy marker so we can ensure you get the right, safe dish.”
6
QR Orders If an order comes through with an allergen message (e.g. from a QR code order), the pass person is responsible for placing a red card stand at the guest's table.
7
Chef's Red Card Chefs place a red card on the tray with the safe dish. The red card stays with the dish until it is handed directly to the guest.
8
Sending the Dish Tell the delivering staff member what the allergen is (e.g. “This dish is nut free”). The allergen must be verbally communicated at the pass.
9
Delivering the Dish Verbally confirm the safe dish with the guest (e.g. “Here is your nut-free ramen”). Remove BOTH the tray red card AND the table red card stand.
10
Return Cards Both the tray red card and the table red card stand are returned to the pass, ready for the next use.

RaRa Ramen Allergy Procedure Checklist

  • Asked guest “Do you have any allergies?”
  • Checked allergy matrix for safe / modifiable dishes
  • Entered allergen notes on ALL dishes including severity
  • Spoke to chef if allergen was unclear or complex
  • Red card stand placed at guest's table
  • QR orders: pass person placed red card stand at table
  • Chef placed red card on tray with safe dish
  • Allergen verbally communicated at the pass
  • Safe dish verbally confirmed with guest on delivery
  • Both red cards returned to the pass

Recognising Allergic Reactions

An allergic reaction can happen very soon after eating and can quickly become life-threatening. All staff must know how to recognise the signs.

Mild to Moderate Reaction Signs

  • Swelling of lips, face, or eyes
  • Hives or welts on the skin
  • Tingling sensation in the mouth
  • Abdominal pain or vomiting

Severe Allergic Reaction (Anaphylaxis) Signs

EMERGENCY — Any of these signs require immediate action:
  • Difficult or noisy breathing
  • Swelling of the tongue
  • Swelling or tightness in the throat
  • Wheeze or persistent cough
  • Difficulty talking or hoarse voice
  • Persistent dizziness or collapse
  • Pale and floppy (especially young children)

How to Help a Customer Having Anaphylaxis

Anaphylaxis is a MEDICAL EMERGENCY It requires immediate treatment with adrenaline (epinephrine). If treatment is delayed, anaphylaxis can be fatal.
1
Lay the person flat — do NOT allow them to stand or walk. If breathing is difficult, allow them to sit on the ground with legs outstretched.
2
Follow the customer's ASCIA Action Plan for Anaphylaxis (if they have one). If available, give an adrenaline (epinephrine) injector (EpiPen). Instructions are on the Action Plan and on the device itself.
3
Call 000 (Triple Zero) immediately. Tell them someone is having anaphylaxis.
4
Stay with the person until the ambulance arrives. Monitor their breathing. Do not leave them alone.
5
Preserve the food the customer was eating. Freeze it and label it — it may be needed for investigation.

Post-Incident Procedure

  • Report the incident internally using the RaRa Ramen incident report form
  • Report near misses as well — these help prevent future incidents
  • Conduct an internal investigation to understand what happened and prevent recurrence
  • Review and update procedures as needed
  • Promote a good food safety culture where staff feel safe reporting concerns

Encouraging Customers to Disclose Allergies

Not all customers will volunteer their allergy information. RaRa Ramen can help by:

  • Including a note on menus asking customers to inform staff of any food allergies
  • Having signage at the counter or ordering area
  • Training staff to ask if customers have any food allergies when taking orders
  • Displaying the allergen menu matrix where customers can see it

Implementing Best Practice at RaRa Ramen

Your Workplace Must Have:

  1. A food allergen management policy covering staff training, allergen knowledge, safe preparation practices, and incident procedures
  2. An allergen menu matrix for all menu items, kept up to date and accessible to all staff
  3. Ingredient lists for all products and recipes, accessible to staff
  4. Food safety monitoring tools that include allergen management in critical control point monitoring
  5. Customer communication procedures including menu notices, verbal communication protocols, and chef card handling
  6. Standardised recipes followed consistently to ensure accurate allergen information
  7. An ordering system to maintain par levels and avoid last-minute ingredient substitutions
  8. An incident reporting procedure for allergic reactions and near misses

Knowledge Check

1. When taking a guest's order, what must you ALWAYS ask?

Correct! You must ALWAYS ask every guest “Do you have any allergies?” when taking their order. Never wait for the guest to volunteer this information.
Incorrect. Step 1 of the RaRa allergy procedure requires you to ALWAYS ask “Do you have any allergies?” every time you take an order.

2. When entering allergen notes on an order, what must you include?

Correct! You must enter the allergen including severity (e.g. “anaphylactic to nuts”) on ALL dishes the guest will have, not just the ones you think are affected.
Incorrect. The allergen note must include severity (e.g. “anaphylactic to nuts”) and must be entered on ALL dishes the guest will have.

3. When delivering a safe dish to the guest, what must you do with the red cards?

Correct! You must verbally confirm the safe dish with the guest, remove BOTH the tray red card and the table red card stand, and return them both to the pass.
Incorrect. You must verbally confirm the safe dish, then remove BOTH red cards (tray card and table stand) and return them to the pass.

4. A customer is showing signs of difficult breathing, swelling of the tongue, and persistent coughing after eating. What should you do?

Correct! These are signs of anaphylaxis. Lay them flat (or seated with legs out if breathing is difficult), use their EpiPen if available, and call 000 immediately. Never allow them to stand or walk.
Incorrect. These are signs of anaphylaxis — a medical emergency. The person must be laid flat (NOT standing), their EpiPen administered if available, and 000 called immediately. Never delay treatment.
